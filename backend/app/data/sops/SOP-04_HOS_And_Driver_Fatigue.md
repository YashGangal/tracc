# Standard Operating Procedure: SOP-04 - Hours-of-Service (HOS) & Driver Fatigue Mitigation

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026  
**Department:** Safety & Compliance  
**Audience:** Dispatchers, Safety Directors, Fleet Operations  

---

## 1. Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) HOS Rules
Dispatchers are legally prohibited from requiring or coercing any property-carrying commercial motor vehicle driver to operate outside statutory limits.

### The 4 Primary Rules:
1. **11-Hour Driving Limit:** May drive a maximum of 11 hours after 10 consecutive hours off duty.
2. **14-Hour Shift Window:** May not drive beyond the 14th consecutive hour after coming on duty, following 10 consecutive hours off duty. Off-duty time does NOT extend the 14-hour period.
3. **30-Minute Driving Break:** Driving is not permitted if more than 8 consecutive hours have passed since the end of the driver's last off-duty or sleeper-berth period of at least 30 minutes.
4. **60/70-Hour Weekly Limit:** May not drive after 60 hours on duty in 7 consecutive days, or 70 hours in 8 consecutive days. A 34-hour restart resets this clock.

## 2. Managing Impending HOS Violations During Dispatches
- When dispatching a load, dispatcher must calculate total route distance divided by **45 mph transit average** plus scheduled dwell times.
- If total required transit time exceeds driver's available driving clock minus 1.5 hours buffer:
  - **Single Driver:** Schedule mandatory 10-hour sleeper berth stop at an authorized truck stop.
  - **Team Driver Assignment:** Convert load to a team driving team if expedited delivery deadline precludes 10-hour shutdown.
  - **Relay / Drop-and-Hook:** Coordinate relay driver at an intermediate terminal.

## 3. Coercion Prevention
Coercion complaints carry civil penalties up to $16,000 per violation. Never instruct a driver to continue driving when their Electronic Logging Device (ELD) warns of an imminent violation.
